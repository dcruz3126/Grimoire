
# **Manifestor Cheat Sheet**

Freedom with responsibility

**Your role is not simply to start things. It's to communicate your vision, take ownership of your commitments, and allow your partner to contribute without feeling controlled.**

## **A. Things to practise regularly**

|Practise|What it looks like|
|---|---|
|Finish what you start|Complete your agreed contribution before moving to the next exciting idea.|
|Inform, don't disappear|Tell your partner what you're doing, what has changed, and when something is delayed.|
|Make your vision understandable|Explain the idea in practical terms, not just how it feels in your head.|
|Receive feedback|Listen without treating every question as an attempt to control you.|
|Respect your partner's pace|Your urgency doesn't automatically become her deadline.|
|Recognise invisible labour|Notice planning, editing, research, organising, and follow-through.|
|Own your commitments|Don't assume your partner will finish what you leave behind.|
|Allow independent creativity|Your partner is a creator, not an assistant assigned to your ideas.|
|Be consistent|Don't change the destination every time a new idea appears.|

## **B. Things to look out for**

These are warning signs to catch before they become recurring arguments.

|Warning sign|Ask yourself|
|---|---|
|I have another exciting idea.|Am I genuinely ready to commit, or am I just excited?|
|I feel restricted by questions.|Is she controlling me, or asking for information she needs to do her part?|
|I want to change the direction.|What work will this undo, and who will have to redo it?|
|I haven't completed my task.|Am I expecting her to pick it up?|
|I feel impatient.|Am I respecting her capacity and process?|
|She disagrees with my vision.|Am I hearing useful feedback as rejection?|
|I feel like she is taking over.|Have I actually communicated what I want, or left a vacuum for her to fill?|
|I feel unappreciated.|Have I recognised what she contributes, too?|

## **C. Scripts to use**

**When you have a new idea**

> "I've got a new idea. I want to share it, but I'm not asking us to commit to it yet. Can we put it in the idea bank and revisit it when we review our current projects?"

**When you need creative freedom**

> "I need room to explore this creatively. I'll take responsibility for the initial concept and bring you something concrete to assess. I want your input, but I don't want us to get stuck planning every detail before we've created anything."

**When you disagree with your Projector**

> "I hear what you're saying. I need a little time to process it. I'm not dismissing your input; I just need to work out what I agree with and what I want to do differently."

**When you've fallen behind**

> "I haven't completed my part, and I don't want you to have to carry it. I can realistically finish it by [date]. If that doesn't work for the project, let's adjust the timeline or scope."

**When you want to change direction**

> "I've had a new thought about the project. Before we change anything, let's look at what we've already invested, what would need to change, and whether this is genuinely an improvement."

**When you feel your partner is over-managing**

> "I appreciate the structure you're bringing. I also need some room to make decisions in my creative area. Can we clarify which decisions are yours to guide and which ones I need to own?"

**When you need to acknowledge her work**

> "I can see that you've put a lot of work into the structure and planning. I don't want that work to become invisible just because I'm focused on the creative side. Let's make sure the responsibilities and recognition are fair."

## **D. Redirections when you catch yourself slipping**

|Instead of…|Redirect to…|
|---|---|
|Starting another project|Finish, pause, or formally release an existing one.|
|Expecting her to understand your vision|Write a one-page creative brief.|
|Defending every creative decision|Ask what problem her feedback is trying to solve.|
|Changing the project halfway through|Put the new idea in the parking lot.|
|Leaving tasks unfinished|Renegotiate before the deadline passes.|
|Expecting immediate action|Ask about her capacity and agree on a realistic date.|
|Treating questions as restrictions|Clarify what information she needs and why.|

## **E. Questions to ask yourself regularly**

- Am I creating something, or just generating possibilities?
    
- What have I actually completed this week?
    
- Have I communicated my intentions clearly?
    
- Am I expecting my partner to translate my ideas into reality?
    
- Have I given her enough information to do her part?
    
- Am I respecting the commitments we've made?
    
- Am I open to her expertise, even when it challenges my first instinct?
    
- Have I recognised her contribution?
    
- Am I allowing her to have her own creative direction?
    
- If I were working alone, what would I have to do myself that I'm currently expecting her to do?
    

## **F. Questions to ask your Projector**

- "What do you need from me to move your part forward?"
    
- "Am I giving you enough clarity, or am I leaving you to figure things out?"
    
- "Are you feeling like you're carrying too much?"
    
- "Where do you think I'm creating unnecessary work?"
    
- "Is there something I'm doing that makes it difficult for you to contribute?"
    
- "Are you giving me feedback because something genuinely needs addressing, or is there another approach you'd like me to consider?"
    
- "Do you feel you have enough room to develop your own ideas?"
    

# **Projector Cheat Sheet**

Insight with boundaries

**Your role is not to make everything work. It's to contribute your insight, structure, and expertise without becoming responsible for another person's commitments.**

You can be strategic without becoming the unpaid project manager of every idea that enters the room.

## **A. Things to practise regularly**

|Practise|What it looks like|
|---|---|
|Stop rescuing|Let your partner experience the consequences of her own unfinished commitments.|
|Ask before advising|Check whether she wants feedback, brainstorming, or simply someone to listen.|
|Separate guidance from ownership|Giving advice doesn't mean taking responsibility for implementing it.|
|Avoid analysis paralysis|Create a small version and learn from the result.|
|Allow creative differences|Not every idea needs to be developed your way.|
|Make your labour visible|Track your time, tasks, decisions, and contributions.|
|Communicate needs directly|Don't expect your partner to notice you're overwhelmed.|
|Protect your own creativity|Develop your own products, ideas, and income streams.|
|Release perfectionism|A finished imperfect prototype is often more useful than a perfect plan.|
|Set boundaries early|Don't wait until you're resentful to say no.|

## **B. Things to look out for**

|Warning sign|Ask yourself|
|---|---|
|I'm doing everything.|Did I agree to this, or did I automatically take over?|
|I can see all the problems.|Do these problems need solving now, or can we test the idea first?|
|She isn't listening.|Did she ask for my input? Have I explained my concern clearly?|
|I feel frustrated by her process.|Is it genuinely damaging the project, or is it simply different from mine?|
|I'm chasing her for work.|Why am I managing a commitment that belongs to her?|
|I feel unappreciated.|Have I communicated what I need, or am I hoping she'll notice?|
|I keep fixing things.|What would happen if I stopped fixing them?|
|I haven't worked on my own ideas.|Have I made her projects more important than my own?|
|I'm trying to prevent every mistake.|Is this a reasonable risk for her to take herself?|

## **C. Scripts to use**

**When your partner presents a new idea**

> "I can see the potential. Before I start developing it, can we clarify whether this is an idea we're exploring or a project we're actually committing to?"

**When you're being given too much responsibility**

> "I'm happy to contribute to this, but I can't be responsible for the entire execution. I need you to own your agreed tasks, and I need us to divide the work before we proceed."

**When you want to give feedback**

> "Would you like me to listen, brainstorm with you, or give you my honest strategic assessment?"

**When you disagree with the vision**

> "I understand what you're aiming for. My concern is that the current direction may not achieve that outcome. Can we compare the two approaches against the audience, purpose, and end result rather than debating which idea feels better?"

**When you're tempted to take over**

> "I can see a way to solve this, but I don't want to automatically take responsibility for it. Would you like my suggestion, or would you prefer to work through it yourself?"

**When your partner misses a commitment**

> "Your part hasn't been completed, and I can't absorb it without affecting my own work. What is your plan for completing it? Until we agree on a new plan, my part will remain paused."

**When you need recognition**

> "I want to make sure we're recognising the work that goes into this beyond the visible creative output. The planning, editing, research, and systems are substantial contributions, and I want those reflected in our agreements."

**When you need to protect your own creative time**

> "I need to dedicate time to my own projects. I won't be available to develop or organise additional ideas until I've completed my own agreed priorities."

## **D. Redirections when you catch yourself slipping**

|Instead of…|Redirect to…|
|---|---|
|Taking over an unfinished task|Ask the owner for a completion plan.|
|Giving unsolicited advice|Ask permission first.|
|Explaining every possible problem|Identify the one risk that matters most right now.|
|Trying to perfect the concept|Build a small prototype.|
|Chasing for updates|Establish a reporting deadline and let the owner report.|
|Getting frustrated that she does things differently|Check whether the outcome is actually compromised.|
|Quietly building resentment|State your boundary and request clearly.|
|Spending all your time on shared work|Schedule protected time for your own IP.|
|Trying to make her see your point|Explain it once, establish the consequence, and let her decide where she has authority.|

## **E. Questions to ask yourself regularly**

- Am I helping, or am I rescuing?
    
- Is this actually my responsibility?
    
- Did she ask for my input?
    
- Am I attached to the best outcome, or to my preferred method?
    
- Am I overcomplicating something that could be tested?
    
- Have I clearly stated what I need?
    
- Am I expecting recognition without communicating my contribution?
    
- Am I allowing my own creative work to suffer?
    
- Am I trying to control the outcome because I fear what happens if I don't?
    
- If I stopped managing this project, would it still be my partner's responsibility to move it forward?
    

## **F. Questions to ask your Manifestor**

- "What part of this project are you personally committing to?"
    
- "What do you need me to take responsibility for, specifically?"
    
- "Is this a new idea or a genuine change to our current direction?"
    
- "What are you willing to complete before we move on?"
    
- "Do you understand what changing this direction will mean for the work already done?"
    
- "Are you comfortable with me giving you honest feedback, even if you don't agree?"
    
- "How can we make sure my contribution is recognised and accounted for?"
    
- "Are you making space for my creative ideas, too?"
    

# **Partnership Cheat Sheet**

This is where the two individual cheat sheets become a working partnership. Both of you need to follow these rules. Neither person's Human Design type gives them automatic authority.

## **A. Five rules to protect the partnership**

### **01**

**Ideas are not commitments.**

An idea only becomes a project when you agree on its purpose, owner, responsibilities, scope, and deadline.

### **02**

**No silent rescuing.**

Nobody automatically takes over another person's unfinished work. Reassigning work requires an explicit agreement.

### **03**

**No endless vision debates.**

If you cannot agree, each person creates a small concept or prototype. Compare the results instead of debating indefinitely.

### **04**

**Both people get creative ownership.**

One person's ideas do not automatically become the company's priorities. Both have room to create independent intellectual property.

### **05**

**Shared consequences require shared decisions.**

Money, ownership, major scope changes, legal commitments, and launch decisions need explicit agreement.

## **B. Who decides what?**

A practical starting point:

| Decision                            | Primary authority                |
| ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Initial creative spark              | Manifestor                       |
| Exploring a new concept             | Manifestor, with Projector input |
| Structure and organisation          | Projector                        |
| Research and feasibility assessment | Projector                        |
| Editorial recommendations           | Projector                        |
| Final creative choices              | Agreed project owner             |
| Changing shared project scope       | Both                             |
| Budget and spending                 | Both                             |
| IP ownership and licensing          | Both                             |
| Launch approval                     | Both                             |
| Individual projects and products    | Individual owner                 |

**Important:** Primary authority does not mean unlimited authority. If a decision affects shared money, workload, reputation, or ownership, both people need a say.

## **C. The weekly 20-minute partnership check-in**

Do this once a week. Keep it short and factual.

**Weekly check-in**

0/6 discussed

**What did I complete this week?**

Name actual finished deliverables, not just activity.

**What am I responsible for next?**

One clear owner and a realistic deadline for each task.

**What am I waiting on from my partner?**

Identify dependencies without taking over.

**Is the workload still fair?**

Include invisible work: research, editing, planning, admin.

**Has the vision or scope changed?**

Decide whether to keep, pause, or formally change direction.

**What do I need to say that I've been holding back?**

Raise concerns before they turn into resentment.

Reset for next meeting

## **D. The disagreement protocol**

When you get stuck on a vision, don't keep circling the same argument.

Use this sequence:

1. **State the shared objective.**
    
    What are we actually trying to create? Who is it for? What should it achieve?
    
2. **Explain your individual vision.**
    
    Each person gets uninterrupted time to explain their concept.
    
3. **Identify the real disagreement.**
    
    Is it about the audience, message, format, quality, workload, money, or creative control?
    
4. **Create two small versions if necessary.**
    
    Don't spend weeks arguing about something you can test in a few days.
    
5. **Choose a temporary direction.**
    
    Commit for 7–14 days. No reopening the decision unless there's a material problem.
    
6. **Review evidence, not egos.**
    
    What worked? What didn't? What did you learn? Decide what happens next.
    

## **E. Scripts for difficult partnership moments**

**When both are getting frustrated**

> "I think we're repeating ourselves. Let's stop trying to convince each other and identify exactly what we're disagreeing about."

**When one person feels unheard**

> "Before we move on, I want to make sure you've understood what I'm actually saying. Can you tell me what you think my concern is?"

**When one person is carrying too much**

> "The current division of work isn't sustainable. We need to redistribute responsibilities or reduce the scope. We can't keep solving the imbalance by expecting one person to do more."

**When the project has stalled**

> "We've spent too much time trying to agree on the perfect version. Let's define the smallest version we can create and test."

**When a new idea threatens an existing project**

> "Let's capture that idea, but we're not changing the current project until we've reviewed the impact on time, workload, and the original goal."

**When you need to pause a collaboration**

> "I value the partnership, but the way we're working right now isn't working for me. I want to pause this project, clarify responsibilities, and agree on a better way forward before continuing."

# **5. The things you should both remember**

There are a few distinctions that matter more than your Human Design labels.

|Manifestor needs to remember|Projector needs to remember|
|---|---|
|Independence isn't exemption from accountability.|Insight isn't ownership of someone else's work.|
|Starting isn't finishing.|Seeing what needs doing doesn't mean doing it.|
|Feedback isn't automatically control.|Disagreement isn't automatically disrespect.|
|A new idea doesn't erase an old commitment.|A better method isn't always the only acceptable method.|
|Your partner isn't your support department.|Your partner isn't your responsibility to manage.|
|Communicate before things become problems.|State boundaries before resentment builds.|

## **The monthly relationship audit**

Once a month, ask each other these six questions and answer honestly:

1. Do I feel respected as an equal partner?
    
2. Is the workload fair, including the work nobody sees?
    
3. Am I able to express disagreement without fear of an argument?
    
4. Are we completing things, or mostly discussing and planning them?
    
5. Do I have room to develop my own creative ideas?
    
6. Is this partnership helping me grow, or am I sacrificing my own goals to keep it functioning?
    

**One final rule:** Don't measure the partnership by how many ideas you generate, how much potential you see, or how exciting the vision feels. Measure it by what you actually create, how fairly you work together, and whether both of you can sustain the arrangement.

You are trying to build a creative business, not a full-time job in managing each other's personalities. The business needs systems; the relationship needs boundaries; the products need finishing. All three matter.
